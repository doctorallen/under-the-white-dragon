# CLAUDE.md

This file provides guidance to Claude Code (claude.ai/code) when working with code in this repository.

Under the White Dragon (it grew out of Downlevel, a detective game; the repo is still named `underhill`, an older game) is a side-scrolling cyberpunk game for the phone browser, held sideways, in plain JavaScript: no build step, no dependencies, no modules. You live in an apartment on floor 31 of a megablock in a vertical city, take the elevator down to the Stack, and go down level by level: strip machines for salvage, cut through grates and shutters, jack into data nodes for programs, sell to vendors, take cases from the lieutenant at the precinct, fly your spinner, get gadgets from the quartermaster and gear from the fabricator, work cases (clues, leads, the baseline test, a verdict on a suspect who may be a synth), and fight drones, chromed gangs and bosses down to the Root. Implants are illegal in this world: the detective carries gadgets, never ware. The game is becoming one about fixing things: you live in a hidden basement under the White Dragon, a noodle bar (`d_dragon.js`: the street, the bar, the storeroom and its trapdoor, and the basement `d_cellar`, where new games start; it's `hidden`, so not on the city map), take jobs to your bench (`bench.js`), use parts with a grade and a history (`parts.js`), and watch your heat (`jobs.js`); its design is in the doc linked from `DESIGN.md`. There's no gun and there are no enemies: Fire is gone, levels' enemies aren't spawned (`Actors.load`, kept for `LEGACY`), and the touch controls are a stick, Use and Jump (`body.legacy` brings back health, RAM, Item, Program and the takedown button). The detective's cases, side jobs, warrants, the precinct and the old apartment are still in the code but switched off by `LEGACY` (`game.js`): `Cases.actionsFor` and `Quests.actionsFor` offer nothing and `Travel.open` keeps those places shut. Ranged gear can't be got (`Game.giveGear`). Read `README.md` for the file map and controls, `js/tuning.js` for every number, `js/items.js` for what you carry, gear, the fabricator, gadgets and programs, `js/cases.js` for the detective's cases, `js/quests.js` for jobs, and `DESIGN.md` for the design. Sections of `DESIGN.md` marked **(proposed)** aren't built yet. Ask before building on them in a way that's hard to undo.

## Commands

```sh
python3 -m http.server 8000     # play at http://localhost:8000
npm install                     # test tools only (puppeteer-core)
npm test                        # everything CI runs: test/check.sh, then test/smoke.js
```

`test/smoke.js` uses puppeteer-core with `$CHROME`. Where that isn't installed it falls back to the `playwright` package's Chromium.

## Architecture

- **Globals and load order.** Every file is a classic script in one global scope, loaded in order by `index.html` (`util.js` first, `main.js` last). A new file needs its `<script>` tag and a line in `sw.js`'s `FILES` (`test/check.sh` fails on either missing, and on duplicate top-level names).
- **Installable, never stale.** `manifest.webmanifest` and `sw.js` make it a PWA. The service worker goes to the network first for everything (revalidating past the HTTP cache) and uses its cache only offline. The deploy stamps `__BUILD__` in `sw.js` and `js/pwa.js` (`PAGE_BUILD`) with the commit and writes `version.json`, so every deploy is a new worker; `js/pwa.js` checks for one whenever the game returns to the foreground and every 15 minutes, and when it takes over, saves and reloads, unless the page is already that build (a fresh launch after a deploy loads the new code from the network, so it mustn't reload again). Don't make the worker cache-first.
- **Levels are code.** Each level in `levels.js` is built once through the builder in `world.js` (`newLevel`): painting operations (`L.paint`, `L.block`, `L.sign`, `L.tube`…), solid boxes (`L.solid`, `L.wall`, `L.floor`), one-way surfaces (`L.walk` walkways, `L.stairs`, stood on from above only), ladders, props, lights, people (`L.npc`, with a daily schedule) and enemies. `World.load(id)` makes it the current level. A save keeps only what changed: props done by id (`Game.s.done`), flags, what you carry (`pack`) and wear (`gear`, with levels), gadgets, programs, cases, jobs, rank and the time (`save.js`, localStorage `whitedragon.save.v1`, save version 1; `Save.start` deletes Downlevel's old save, `downlevel.save.v1`, and starts over from any save of another version).
- **Seeded.** Painting and sprites use `hash` and `Rng` only, never `Math.random` (enforced by `check.sh`), so the city always paints the same.
- **Painting.** `paint.js` paints the level lazily in chunks (`T.chunk` px square). A chunk runs only the operations whose box touches it; each operation gets its own seed (`op.seed`), so strokes meet across chunk edges without seams. Painting is flat (`PAINT_FLAT` in `paint.js`): each surface one tone from its value, a crisp lit top edge and shaded bottom (`blockV`), panel lines, drips, no noise and no brushwork. (Set it false and the old way comes back: short strokes of close tones, `strokeRow`, and bevelled lips.) Behind it a level can have parallax layers (`L.layers`, back to front, each painted once and moved at its fraction `f` of the camera's speed); `skyline.js` paints their towers (`tower`, `skylineLayer`, `cloudBands`) and draws what moves on them each frame: giant holograms (`L.holos`) and the monorail train (`L.train`). A chunk is a colour canvas plus a glow canvas.
- **Rendering** (`render.js`). The view is small (`T.view.min` px on the short side) and scaled up by whole device pixels: the frame is finished at `View.out` screen pixels per view pixel (at most 3, lower when `Perf` in `main.js` sees frames under 40 a second, or as the dev setting says) and CSS scales the canvas the rest of the way, pixelated. People are lit by tinting their sprite, and the tinted copy is cached on the sprite by quantised colour (`s.lit`), so a figure costs one draw a frame; don't go back to compositing through a shared scratch canvas per figure, which stalls the GPU. Order: far layer, chunks (each erasing the far layer's glow behind it), props, then the light map (`lightMap`, a 4 px grid fixed to the world, scaled up smooth) multiplied over all of it (each light adds itself only over the cells it reaches). Keep it smooth: hard light cells read as a grid over the screen. People and enemies are lit after that, each by the light where they stand with a floor (`lightAt`), so figures always read. Then the parts of props in front of people (`p.front`) and rain. The 2D canvases stop there: `View.low` (the lit picture) and `View.gl` (what glows) go to `gl.js`, which finishes the frame in WebGL2: the glow halved by averaging and blurred into two bloom layers (averaging keeps the bloom steady as the camera moves), then picture + glow + bloom, wet reflections (`L.wet`) and the fade, scaled up to the screen. Without WebGL2, or with `?nogl` in the address, `render.js` finishes it in 2D; the smoke test plays the desktop run that way.
- **Sprites** are painted from shapes (`Spr` in `spr.js`: `ell`, `box`, `cap`, `shape`, `limb`, `poly`) and cached. Every sprite is flat pixel art by default, props and figures alike (`Spr.soft` gives the old soft-run shading if something needs it): three hard tones a material and no noise, a box a flat panel (lit top and left edge, shaded bottom and right), a shadow line where a later part (`S.part()`) lies over an earlier one (`S.part(false)` for one that casts none, like hair on a face), and a dark outline, with the details (eyes, a coat's opening and buttons, a buckle, seams and rivets) placed by hand. Keep everything flat: no soft volume shading, no bevels or embossing. People come from `person(o)` in `people.js` (hair, coat, chrome, what they hold, a pose). The runner's gear and gadgets show on the sprite (`RunnerArt`). Sprites carry anchors `ax`/`ay` at the feet.
- **Play.** `player.js` moves the runner against the level and works Use (`Player.interact`: the nearest prop or person; salvage and cutting are held). Props' kinds, looks and uses are in `things.js`. People and enemies are in `actors.js`; enemies notice you before they fight (`Foes.notice`: an awareness meter, facing, light from the light map, how fast you move; `Foes.alarm`, `Foes.noise` for shots, quiet takedowns from behind with `Foes.canTakedown`). Jacking in is `hack.js` (`Hack`: a network made from the node's place, crossed a node at a time against a trace; drawn as HTML in the `net` panel; every district gets a terminal, `term: true`, from `district`). `life.js` is the city's ambient life: light flicker (`L.sign`/`L.tube`/`L.fire` lights carry a `flick` mode), adverts (`L.ad`), flying traffic on the far layer (`L.traffic`), critters (`L.critter`), litter (`L.litter`), smoke and steam (`L.vents`), drips (`L.drips`); it's all driven by the clock and `hash`, so none of it is saved. Passers-by come from `crowd()` in `levels.js`. `Game` (`game.js`) has the rules: what you carry (no limit, and nothing is lost on death) and payment, gear (`GEAR` in `items.js`: a piece a slot, Cyberpunk-style head, face, outer and inner torso, legs, feet, ranged and melee; armour, weapon stats and perks come from what's worn, and its `look` dresses the runner's sprite), jobs, the clock, moving between levels (`Game.go`), damage and death.
- **Cases and jobs.** `cases.js` has the cases (`CASES`, state in `Game.s.cases`): clues (items, photos, a witness's story and the truth they hid, `kind: 'truth'`, got by presenting the clue in `by`), leads worked out on the case board (`Board` in `panels.js`, `Cases.deduce`: a question, three answers, the first written is right, and exactly the `needs` pinned; a wrong call adds doubt), a suspect, a verdict; the night desk generates one from the day (`Cases.nightOffer`); `Baseline` is the test; the photo enhancer is `Enhancer` in `enhance.js` (a camera still of the scene with a few spots too small to read, one the clue's detail and the rest decoys; zoom in on one and it sharpens into its own little picture). `quests.js` has jobs (`QUESTS`, steps go/talk/collect/kill/deliver/choose/use/hack, state in `Game.s.quests`), named elites and the warrants board; `Quests.event` hears kills and uses, `Quests.tick` checks the rest. Neither puts anything in a level's code: `World.questProps` adds the clue and pickup props, and `Actors.questSpawns`/`caseNpcs` add witnesses, suspects, elites and fugitives, whenever a case or job moves on. Their panels are in `panels.js` (`PANELS`, bound by `panelBind`).
- **Places and travel.** Every place is built by hand. `kit.js` holds the pieces (buildings `bShophouse`, `bCapsules`, `bMachiya`, `bTemple`, `bPagoda`, `bGlass`, `bStack`, `bDanchi`, `bTheater`, `bBath`, `bArcade`, `bPark`; painters `kRoof`, `kLattice`, `kNoren`, `kLantern`, `kAwning`, `kBalcony`, `kWin`, `kShop`, `kTree`, `kTorii`...; stalls `kStallAt`) and `district(o)`, the frame a place is built in (ground, the city behind or a ceiling, the way in, crowd, light), with `addDistrict` to register it and `kFoes` to place enemies. The places are in `d_high.js`, `d_street.js`, `d_under.js`, `d_old.js`, `d_works.js`, `d_deep.js`; each file's own helpers carry its prefix (`hi`, `st`, `un`, `ot`, `wk`, `dp`). From the ground the camera shows about G-155 to G, so keep the detail that matters low. Every level has `metres` (its depth) and `mapX`; `worldmap.js` pins them on the city map, which is how you travel, and `Travel` (`travel.js`) decides what's open (keys and where you've been) and how you get there (spinner, manhole, subway, lift cage). The city layers behind sky places are painted once and shared (`World.shared`). Enemies are defined in `enemies.js` (`ENEMY`, AI and art in `Foes`).
- **The basement.** `Bench` (`bench.js`) is a panel with its own canvas (`BW` × `BH` bench pixels, drawn flat and scaled up) and a side of HTML it redraws alone (`Bench.side`); what's on the bench is saved (`Game.s.bench`), the tools' state in hand isn't. Devices and parts are data in `parts.js` (`DEVICE`, `PART`, `GRADE`, `PART_STOCK`); parts you have are instances in `Game.s.parts`, the notebook is `Game.s.notes`. `Jobs` (`jobs.js`) puts its customers into the White Dragon (`Jobs.spawn`, from `Actors.questSpawns`), talks through the talk panel (`Jobs.actionsFor`, `Jobs.html`), keeps heat (`Game.s.heat`) and writes the morning after when you sleep (`Jobs.morning`).
- **The block and its people.** The White Dragon's street (`d_dragon`) has doors (`door` props) into hidden interiors, and each interior has its way out drawn on its back wall where you come in, often mid-room (`innerDoor` in `props.js`, `look` noren/glass/steel; `TOWN_PLACE.inside` matches it): the noodle bar (`d_noodles`, in `d_dragon.js`), Mo's grocery and the flats (`d_block.js`). Walking off the end of any district takes you to the next one at the same depth (`Travel.neighbours`, `Travel.edge`; spawns `west`/`east`); spinner pads are cab stands (a fare, free once Ozzy owes you). The residents (`RESIDENTS` in `town.js`) each have a home, a job, friends and a day plan; `Town.init` expands errands and spaces the plan, `Town.state(id, hour)` gives where someone is from the clock alone (a route out of one door, along the street, in another, up the stairs), `Town.sync` keeps the ones in your level among `Actors.npcs` (`def.town`, moved by Town, not by `sched`), and `Town.chatter`/`arrived`/`talk` do greetings, small talk, the till and what they tell you. Nothing about them is saved.
- **Flying.** `flight.js`: a flight is its own mode; while `Flight.on`, `step` runs only the flight and `render` draws it (its own painted copy of `cityLayers`) before the usual finish (`finishFrame`), then `Game.arrive`.
- **Map and dev.** `map.js` paints every chunk of the level into one picture (cached per level) for the pause map; `dev.js` holds testing switches in localStorage `downlevel.dev` (god mode in `Game.hurt`, speed in `Player`, the clock in `step`).
- **Time.** `step(dt)` (`main.js`) is one fixed 1/60 s step. Panels and jacking in set `Game.paused`.

## The look of the menus

Menus and the HUD are a game's, not a web page's: Dragon Pixel (`fonts/dragon.woff`, our own font, built by `python3 tools/mkfont.py fonts/dragon.woff` from the glyphs in that script; add a glyph there if text needs one) at 15px for text and 20px for titles and buttons; windows and cards with cut corners (`--cut`, `--cut-s` in `css/style.css`) over the dimmed game; chunky buttons (`.go` the main one). The skin is the last section of `css/style.css`. Anything a job needs is shown as a checklist with the part's picture (`Jobs.needs`, `Parts.icon`, `Parts.sources`).

## Testing changes

`node test/smoke.js` plays through the slice headlessly on a phone and a desktop: it steps the game with `step(1/60)` and drives the controls through `Input.bot` and the edge flags (`Input.useHit`, `strikeHit`…), and writes screenshots to `test/out/` (gitignored). Look at them for visual changes. `?seed` isn't used; the levels are fixed.

## Conventions

- Comments and in-game text are plain English. Match the surrounding density.
- Commits go to `main` with short sentence-case subjects. Every push to `main` deploys to GitHub Pages once the checks pass.
